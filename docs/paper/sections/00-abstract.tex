\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines increasingly split documents at their headings, so
the heading hierarchy that a PDF parser recovers decides what each chunk contains and which section
path it carries. We describe \sysname, a heading-hierarchy extractor for born-digital PDFs that
works from the PDF text layer and its typography rather than from page images. It has three
settings of increasing cost. \emph{Free} uses no model: it anchors the PDF's bookmarks or its
printed table of contents to body lines and fills the gaps with typography and numbering rules.
\emph{Quick check} sends only candidate heading lines to a small language model. \emph{Standard}
lets a model read every line in windows and quote verbatim start and end markers, which a
deterministic resolver maps back to exact character ranges, rejecting table-of-contents copies and
flagging anything it cannot verify. On READoc, scored with the benchmark's own Heading Tree TEDS
code, Standard with a small model scores 70.0 on 905 arXiv papers that were not used during
development, against 49.4 for Docling 2.140 with its heading-hierarchy stage enabled. On the same
papers, MinerU with its own LLM title-levelling step on a larger model scores 64.8. On GitHub READMEs there is no such gain:
Docling is level with or ahead of our settings. The model settings cost about \$0.07 (Quick check)
and \$0.16 (Standard) per 1,000 pages with a small model. Without bookmarks, the free setting is
weaker than Docling on papers, and nothing here works on scanned pages. In a small FinanceBench
pilot (51 questions), chunks split at headings retrieved the evidence page more often than
fixed-size chunks, but our headings did no better than MinerU's. We report the development
history behind each number, including a thread-safety bug that silently dropped bookmarks.
